# Power

Powering off (System OFF or ship mode), the nPM1300 PMIC on the Control Board, and the surge when the pointing stick switches on. Current measurements: [roadmap](roadmap.md) (idle current, pointing stick power) and [Keyboard power](https://github.com/Ziemniaczenka/ReThink_Keyboard/blob/main/docs/keyboard_power.md) in the hardware repository.

## Power off

`src/power.c` switches the stick, the adapter, the backlight and all LEDs off, then enters System OFF (about 2 µA on the profiler with the T420 and its adapter, 2026-11-13; 37 µA with the E580 while KBD_BL_DTCT rested on its pull-up, which the keyboard grounds: about 35 µA, see the [roadmap](roadmap.md)), or the nPM1300's ship mode, where everything is off, BUCK2 included, and only USB wakes the board. Plugging in USB always wakes the board, which then boots afresh. Four reasons:

- **Wired mode without USB:** with the switch on wired and no USB power, after 3 s. Moving the switch to Bluetooth wakes it too; the pairing button and the keys do nothing.
- **Low battery:** on battery, under 3.3 V in three readings 10 s apart (`CONFIG_RETHINK_LOW_BATTERY_OFF_MV`): ship mode (`CONFIG_RETHINK_LOW_BATTERY_SHIP_MODE`), so a stray key can't drain the cell further. The voltage comes from framework-cb's fuel gauge rather than its level, which depends on the battery model. A T420 runs with its stick from a good cell at 3.15 V, so 3.3 V leaves margin.
- **Unused:** on battery, no input from the keys, the stick or the buttons for 30 minutes (`CONFIG_RETHINK_IDLE_SLEEP_MIN`). A key, USB or moving the mode switch wakes it; the key that wakes it isn't typed. Keys on `rethink,matrix` keyboards wake it, since its idle matrix waits on level interrupts, which arm the GPIO sense.
- **Resets in a row on battery:** a cell too weak for the board (worn, cold or nearly empty) can reset it as it starts, again and again, a few mA on average, and the low-battery readings never add up. Each start on battery is counted in flash; 30 s up, or any power-off above, clears the count, and a start on USB power doesn't count. A start that finds 3 in a row (`CONFIG_RETHINK_RESET_LOOP_OFF`) goes to ship mode at once, before the stick and Bluetooth start. Two flash writes per start on battery. A source too weak for the board to get that far isn't caught.

The same battery readings keep BUCK2 out of dropout: 3.0 V under 3.55 V, 3.3 V again over 3.65 V ([BUCK2 in dropout](#npm1300-on-the-control-board)).

In the shell: `power stat` (USB power, battery voltage and cut-off, BUCK2's voltage, the resets in a row before this start, or why the board was in ship mode before it; time unused), `power lowbat <mV>`, `power buck2 <down> <up>` (`0 0` leaves BUCK2 alone) and `power sleep <min>` (until the next reset, to try them without draining the cell or waiting), `power off` (sleep now).

## nPM1300 on the Control Board

Found while profiling from a 3.3 V supply (2026-11-11/12), on a bare Control Board on a bench supply and with the adapter and an E580. The board runs the nRF, its LEDs and the adapter from BUCK2 and the backlight switch from BUCK1; the nPM1300 starts both at power-up from their VSET resistors (330 kΩ: BUCK2 3.3 V, BUCK1 2.7 V). Measured in series with the supply; test points VO1 and VO2 are the two buck outputs.

- **BUCK1 was never switched off.** Framework's board files mark it `regulator-boot-off`, but Zephyr only switches off a buck it reads as on, from BUCKSTATUS's power-good bit, and that bit reads 0 for a running BUCK1 (`regulator is_enabled BUCK1`: disabled, VO1 3.34 V); Zephyr even sets it to 3.3 V. Below about 3.4 V input it sits in dropout, about 3 mA. Fix: `src/pmic.c` switches it off at boot with an enable and a disable (`CONFIG_RETHINK_PMIC`). Worth reporting to Framework (FrameworkComputer/zephyr `framework-wireless-boards`, last changed 2026-10-08, has nothing on it).
- **BUCK2 stuck after a voltage change without headroom.** BUCK2 whose voltage is changed from an input below about 3.35 V stays in PWM mode with its power good at 0: BUCKSTATUS 0x20 or 0x28 instead of 0x40 or 0x48, about 3 mA more, System OFF included, until the input rises above about 3.35 V or USB comes. First seen as 4.8 mA from 3.3 V and 3.2 V (1.6 mA at 3.8 V, 1.8 mA at 3.4 V), with Zephyr setting BUCK2 to 3.3 V at boot (`regulator-init-microvolt`), the voltage its resistor gives anyway (dropout alone draws as much, next item). Without that write a start from 3.2 V left BUCK2 fine; a change to 3.0 V from 3.16 V then stuck it (BUCKSTATUS 0x08 before, 0x28 after). The same change on a running board whose cell dropped from 3.7 V to 3.2 V within 10 s didn't (once), nor did one from a cell at 3.16 V with its power good at 0 (2026-11-13, BUCKSTATUS 0x80 to 0x40): not every such change. Lowering BUCK2 further or forcing its hysteretic mode doesn't clear it; switching it off and on does (0x28 to 0x48, 4.8 to 1.9 mA). Nordic's [anomaly 27](https://docs.nordicsemi.com/bundle/errata_nPM1300_Rev1/page/ERR/nPM1300/Rev1/latest/anomaly_300_27.html) ("Programming BUCK voltage increases current consumption", 1 mA) has the same trigger, and PebbleOS clears it the same way ([PebbleOS #2522](https://github.com/coredevices/PebbleOS/pull/2522)); the errata say nothing of the headroom. Fix (`CONFIG_RETHINK_BUCK2_RESTART`): the shields delete `regulator-init-microvolt` (`rethink_cb.dtsi`); from a cell under 3.55 V the boot sets BUCK2 to 3.0 V (next item) and then switches it off and on, so it starts again at 3.0 V with headroom; at boot and at each battery check (every 10 s, 20 ms after a change) a BUCK2 in PWM mode is switched off and on. The nRF and the adapter run through the gap on the rail's capacitors.
- **BUCK2 in dropout.** From an input under about 3.35 V, BUCK2 at 3.3 V can't regulate and passes the input through: BUCKSTATUS 0x80 (power good 0, the PWM bit set, the mode not PWM: not the stuck state above, so no restart), about 3 mA more, System OFF included. On the EFM32 kit's 3.3 V with the T420 (2026-11-13): connected and idle 4.8 mA, wired mode (System OFF) 3.15 mA, against 1.81 mA and 2.1 µA with BUCK2 at 3.0 V. At 3.4 V it isn't in dropout yet (1.8 mA, above). In System OFF nothing reads the cell, so a board asleep or in wired mode with its cell in that band would drain it at 3 mA down to the cell's own protection. Fix: BUCK2 at 3.0 V under 3.55 V (`CONFIG_RETHINK_BUCK2_LOW_MV`), at 3.3 V again over 3.65 V (`CONFIG_RETHINK_BUCK2_HIGH_MV`), from the battery readings in `src/power.c`; both changes have headroom, except the boot's from a low cell, which the restart above follows. An nRF reset, a wake from System OFF too, keeps what was set; the nPM1300 starts BUCK2 at 3.3 V again only when it powers up (ship mode included). Once on the bench, lowering the input brought the stuck state back somewhere between 3.35 V and 3.02 V, not reproducibly; the battery check's restart covers it. At 3.0 V the white and blue LEDs barely light, the red and green and the keyboards' lock LEDs are dimmer.
- **No nPM1300 access right before System OFF.** Reading BUCK2's voltage in the power-off path made the system timer (GRTC) wake the nRF54L again at once (`ble log`: "the system timer woke it from System OFF"), every 3 s in wired mode, even with all twelve GRTC compare channels disabled; without that read it stays off (bisected: the battery check's pending 1 s timer wasn't it). Cause not understood. Switching the backlight off there (BUCK1) looked the same (2026-11-17): in wired mode with the backlight on, the board woke at once, ZMK restored the backlight, and it powered off again, in a loop. So the power-off path switches the backlight off first, then doesn't touch the nPM1300 and waits 200 ms with the battery check, the `pmic` sampling and the USB-C check stopped; the fuel gauge's own periodic reads remain, none seen to cause it.
- **Left:** BUCK2 isn't lowered on the way into System OFF (above), so a board that went to sleep with its cell over 3.55 V, BUCK2 at 3.3 V, and whose cell then self-discharges under about 3.35 V over months sits in dropout at about 3 mA, with nothing reading the cell. A battery check that finds the cell dropped from over 3.55 V to under 3.35 V within 10 s changes BUCK2 without headroom; the restart follows 20 ms later.
- **USB input limit.** The board files set the nPM1300's USB input limit to 500 mA (`vbus-limit-microamp`), which it applies at each plug-in, and the charge current to 450 mA: about 50 mA is left for the board while it charges at full rate. The backlight (E580: 26 mA from the battery at its low step, 51 mA at its high step) took its share out of the charge current. The nPM1300 reads the USB-C source's offer on CC1/CC2, which the Control Board wires to it. Half a second after USB power comes, an offer of 1.5 A or 3 A raises the limit to 1.5 A, the nPM1300's maximum (`CONFIG_RETHINK_USB_1500MA`); default USB power (a USB-A port, a C-to-A cable) keeps 500 mA. Unplugging returns it to 500 mA. On a USB-C charger (2026-11-17): offer 1.5 A or 3 A, limit 1500 mA, 440 mA into the cell with the backlight on.
- **Battery current.** The nPM1300 read 0 to -1 mA while the board drew 4.8 mA: no use at the board's 1 A discharge range (about 1.1 mA a step; 0.22 mA at 200 mA).
- **Tools:** `pmic` (BUCKSTATUS per buck: mode, power good, PWM; the battery current; the same as last read on battery; BUCK2 at boot: battery, BUCKSTATUS, after setting 3.0 V, after the restart; the restarts since; the USB source's offer, the input limit and whether it is reached now), `power stat` (BUCK2's voltage and thresholds), `power buck2`, the `regulator` shell (`regulator vget BUCK2`).

## Pointing stick switch-on surge

Switching the stick's supply on (TP_3V3, Control Board P3.12: at boot, with Fn+F8, and when a key wakes the stick after its idle power-off) connects the stick's capacitors and the adapter's 10 µF (C3), roughly 10-20 µF, to BUCK2's rail at once. The Control Board's switch Q2 (EM6M2: its N-FET on P3.12 pulls the P-FET's gate down through R19, 1 kΩ; R20, 10 MΩ, pulls it up) turns on within tens of nanoseconds, so that charge, some 30-70 µC, comes from BUCK2 and the battery input within tens of microseconds, at a few hundred mA. A 5 V stick (T420) adds the LM2775 charge pump's start, which charges TP_5V and the stick to 5 V through its doubler, so twice that charge from the input.

- **From a cell** (2026-11-13, T420): a good 1000 mAh cell at 3.15 V, with nothing in series, starts the board with the stick, and Fn+F8 works. Through a multimeter on its mA range the same cell resets the board at the switch-on; on the 10 A range it works. A worn 400 mAh cell (about 350 mΩ) resets the board at the switch-on from 3.45 V and doesn't start it at all at 3.2 V, in Bluetooth and wired mode alike; at 3.39 V the keyboard runs with the stick held off, and Fn+F8 costs one reset.
- **From the bench** (2026-11-12/13): from a bench supply through a multimeter on its mA range, and from the EFM32 kit's 3.3 V, the board resets at the switch-on, starts the stick again and resets again: a boot loop (about 0.57 s with the kit and an E580). The stick itself isn't needed: with the E580's 12-pin cable out, Fn+F8 still resets the board from the kit, so the surge is the board's and the adapter's capacitors behind Q2. Without the stick the kit's boot also looped without end at times: the bootloader switching TP_3V3 on (next item). From the bench supply every start below 3.8 V looped with the E580; the T420 reset the board at the switch-on below about 3.4 V, with or without 100 µF across the battery input, and with the meter on its 10 A range worked at 3.3 V. A meter's mA range puts roughly 1-10 Ω (shunt and fuse) in the line, and a supply's regulation and the kit's current sensing are far too slow for the surge, so the board's small capacitor on VBAT empties and VBAT collapses; a cell's 0.1-0.3 Ω sits right at the connector. An autoranging meter dropping to a µA range puts hundreds of ohms in the line: the likely cause of the loops in wired mode at 3.2 V (the stick isn't switched on there), seen neither on a cell nor on the profiler.
- **At power-up, from the bootloader** (2026-11-16): the prerelease MCUboot on early Control Boards switches TP_3V3 on itself. It was built with the board's `tp-power` regulator (`regulator-boot-on`), so P3.12 is high while it runs: on a scope, and in USB recovery mode. Q2 then connects C3 0.2-0.4 s after the 3.3 V rail comes up, right after the bootloader's wait for the 32 kHz crystal. From a weak source the rail falls at once to about 1.6 V, where TP_3V3 meets it; the nRF resets, BUCK2 stays off about 130 ms, and the bootloader starts again: a boot loop before this firmware runs, so neither the stick guard nor the reset count sees it. A bare Control Board boots every time (no C3 behind Q2); with the adapter, from the EFM32 kit or a worn cell at 3.5 V, it loops. Framework's MCUboot leaves `tp_power` out since 2026-06-23 ([FrameworkComputer/mcuboot](https://github.com/FrameworkComputer/mcuboot), `daisy_kb_dvt1`), so a current bootloader ends it.
- **Firmware** (`src/trackpoint.c`): Q2 can't be switched on gently from P3.12: even the shortest pulse turns it fully on, and R20 keeps it on for hundreds of microseconds, longer than the surge lasts. So the stick is left off at boot:
  - After a switch-on that reset the board (`CONFIG_RETHINK_TRACKPOINT_GUARD`): a switch-on on battery is marked in flash and the mark cleared once the board came through it, whatever the stick did. A board that boots with the mark still there leaves the stick off, the keyboard working, until USB power or Fn+F8 (`tp on`). So a weak source costs one reset, not a loop: seen with the worn cell at 3.45 V, on the EFM32 kit, and on the bench at 3.3 V. Two flash writes per switch-on on battery, one 16-byte entry each: in ZMS's 36 KB settings partition about 1100 switch-ons before a location is written again, so wear is negligible. A reset of another kind during the second or so of a switch-on (watchdog, battery pulled) leaves the stick off too.
  - In wired mode without USB, where the board powers off within 3 s anyway: until USB power comes or the switch goes to Bluetooth. Saves the switch-on at every wake in wired mode.

  `tp stat` shows why the stick is off. Resets in a row from anything else end in ship mode ([Power off](#power-off)).
- **Bench and profiler:** a reservoir capacitor across the battery input at the board: 100 µF carried the E580 to 3.2 V on the bench, the T420 on the EFM32 kit needed 1000 µF. Low-leakage (ceramic or polymer; an electrolytic can leak µA, though one measured 230 nA). 100 µF on BUCK2's output instead (the Control Board's nPM_out2, or LED_3V3 on the adapter) stops the board from starting at all (0.8 mA, steady). Or a meter on its 10 A range, or bridged with a wire while switching on.
- **Hardware options**, for a next revision, against weak cells, best first (also in the hardware repository's [adapter notes](https://github.com/Ziemniaczenka/ReThink_Keyboard/blob/main/docs/adapter_notes.md#for-a-next-revision)):
  1. **Adapter: a load switch with a set rise time** on TP_3V3 in front of the stick and the pump, e.g. TPS22917 or TPS22918 (SOT-23-6, 1-5.5 V, rise time set by a capacitor on CT; aim for about 1 ms), switched on by its own input. C3 moves behind it, about 1 µF stays in front. The switch-on then draws about 20 µF × 3.3 V / 1 ms ≈ 70 mA. Works from any source, for any stick, on a stock Control Board. For a 5 V stick the pump's own start remains: less capacitance on TP_5V, or the 5 V boost converter with a set soft start that the roadmap already suggests instead of the pump (for efficiency).
  2. ~~**Adapter: a reservoir on LED_3V3**~~ (BUCK2's output): ruled out, with 100 µF there the board doesn't start (above).
  3. **A reservoir on VBAT**, as on the bench: VBAT doesn't reach the adapter (only through Control Board R59, not fitted), so it is a Control Board or battery cable change.
  4. **Control Board: a slower Q2**, a capacitor from its P-FET's gate to its drain (TP_VDD) with a smaller R20 so it still turns off quickly. Rework of Framework's board, not for stock boards.
